## What I need to settle before replying

The responses show interest, but they ask for different things. I need to answer the actual questions before treating every response as a meeting invitation.

The website is still being edited. I will keep its link out of these replies. For someone asking for an example, the next useful item is an approved story outline or a clearly marked concept sample.

These decisions need review:

- **Format:** Do we want the first stories to be written features, with optional photos supplied with permission? This is a proposed starting point, not an approved format. Audio, video and a podcast have not been agreed.
- **Publication and audience:** Where would a finished story appear, and who would see it? The intended community is people interested in whitetail hunting, family, mentorship and outdoor service. I have not established an audience size or guaranteed distribution.
- **Brand purpose:** These stories would be associated with Whitetail Syndicate, a clothing and community brand. I should explain that connection plainly.
- **Participation:** What would I ask of the organizer, hunter, mentor and family? My proposed first step is a short conversation with the organizer, followed by an invitation to a willing storyteller.
- **Permission and review:** Agree on recording, photographs, quotations and publication before using them. Youth participation goes through the organizer and parent or guardian. Decide what the storyteller would see before publication.
- **Time and commitments:** Confirm what I can realistically schedule. Attendance, sponsorship, payment, purchases and event access have not been promised.

A consistent explanation can then be tailored to each person's question. Mention the response volume only when it helps explain scheduling. It should not replace an answer about the project.

## Working story outline to approve

A possible written feature would cover:

1. The person, family or mentor and why this hunt mattered.
2. The preparation and what helped them feel ready.
3. A specific moment from the hunt or camp.
4. What they learned and what they carried home.
5. The organizer or community contribution, where relevant.

This is a proposed structure. It is not a completed story, published example or promise of a particular output. Photos, quotations and any recording would require separate agreement. Publication details remain open.
