Live interview questions
Ask one question at a time. Let them finish. Skip questions they have already answered naturally.

Before recording
Ask: "Are you good with me recording this? We will go over what can be shared before
anything gets posted."
Record a 20 second test and play it back. Check clear speech and reduce nearby noise.
Put phones on Do Not Disturb. Confirm camera framing and available recording space.
Opening: "I want to hear how you got into hunting, then we will get into that hunt. Just tell it how you
remember it."
The hunter
1. How did you get started hunting?
2. Who took you the first time? What do you remember about that?
3. What keeps you going back?
The buildup
4. What hunt are we talking about today? Why does that one stand out?
5. Before that hunt, what did you know about that deer? Had you been watching it, or was this your
first encounter?
6. What had you put into getting ready? Walk me through the scouting and your setup.
7. Take me back to that day. What was happening before you headed out?
8. Where were you sitting, and what were the conditions like?
9. What happened while you were waiting?
Follow an answer
"What happened next?" "Can you describe that?"
"When you say that, what do you mean?"
"You mentioned something earlier. Can we go back to that?"
While they talk: Leave a pause after each answer. Nod instead of talking over them. Their uninterrupted voice will
carry the hunting scenes.

FOR THE INTERVIEWER
The encounter and the ending
Get the sequence clearly. Let the hunter supply the emotion and meaning. Keep uncertain details uncertain.
Craig Ramos / CraigCODA
2 / 5
Slow down at the encounter
10. What was the first thing that made you realize the deer was there?
11. What was it doing? Walk me through what you were seeing.
12. What was going through your head right then?
13. What made you decide to act, wait, or let it go?
14. Take me through what happened next, as closely as you remember it.
15. What did you actually see or hear afterward?
16. What did you do next? Walk me through how the hunt ended.
Follow the outcome
Recovered: "Tell me about finding it. Who was there, and who did you tell first?"
Missed or passed: "What happened to the deer afterward? Did you ever get another chance?"
Not recovered: "Walk me through the search. What could you confirm, and what never got
answered?"
What stayed with them
17. When you think about that hunt now, what moment comes back first?
18. What made it matter to you beyond whether you got the deer?
19. Did it change how you hunted afterward?
20. What happened after that season? Is there more to this story?
21. What have I not asked that you want people to understand?
Before they leave
Complete the details and sharing preferences sheets. Ask what photos or footage exist, what must stay private, and
how to reach them for the edit review.
